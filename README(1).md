# UPSC Civil Services Exam — Syllabus, Wikipedia Links & Resources

A structured, linkable breakdown of the UPSC Civil Services Examination syllabus (Prelims + Mains), with every topic and sub-topic mapped to its corresponding Wikipedia article, plus a curated list of free/open-source tools and reference sources for aspirants.

> **Disclaimer:** This is an unofficial, community-style reference compiled for personal study use. For the authoritative syllabus, always cross-check with [upsc.gov.in](https://upsc.gov.in/exams-related-info/exam-syllabus). Wikipedia links point to standard English article titles — a few niche/administrative terms may not have dedicated pages and are left unlinked or mapped to the closest matching article. Free resource links may change ownership or go behind paywalls over time; verify before relying on them.

## Open Source Software & Other Helpful Resources

*Compiled from official government sources and recurring recommendations across UPSC prep forums/communities (r/UPSC and similar aspirant discussion boards).*

### Open Source / Free Tools

| Tool | Use case |
|---|---|
| [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) / [AnkiDroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AnkiDroid) | Free, open-source spaced-repetition flashcards — the most consistently recommended tool for retaining dates, schemes, and static facts. Build small decks for things you keep forgetting rather than one giant deck. |
| [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/) | Open-source reference/note organizer — useful for keeping tagged notes and PDF sources organized by GS paper/topic. |
| [Joplin](https://joplinapp.org/) | Open-source, end-to-end encrypted note-taking app with markdown support — a free alternative to Evernote/Notion for daily/monthly notes. |
| [LibreOffice](https://www.libreoffice.org/) | Free office suite for answer-writing practice, note formatting, and offline study material. |
| [Standard Notes](https://standardnotes.com/) | Open-source, encrypted, minimal note-taking — good for distraction-free daily current-affairs notes. |
| [Forest](https://www.forestapp.cc/) / [KDE Plasma "Do Not Disturb"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDE_Plasma_5) style app blockers | Focus/distraction-blocking during study blocks — repeatedly mentioned as more useful than adding another content app. |

### Official Government Sources (Free, Primary, Exam-Aligned)

- [PIB — Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in) — official press releases; scheme-based Prelims questions are frequently traceable almost verbatim to PIB releases.
- [PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org) — neutral, plain-language summaries of bills and Parliamentary debates; strong for GS Paper II.
- [PRS Monthly Policy Review](https://prsindia.org/policy/monthly-policy-review) — a free monthly digest summarizing the month's legislative/policy developments.
- [Union Budget & Economic Survey](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in) — essential annual reading for GS Paper III economy questions.
- [UPSC official syllabus & notifications](https://upsc.gov.in/exams-related-info/exam-syllabus) — the notified syllabus PDF itself, not a coaching paraphrase of it.
- [eGyanKosh (IGNOU)](http://egyankosh.ac.in/) — free IGNOU degree-level material for Political Science, History, Economics, Public Administration — useful for Mains depth and several optionals.
- [NIOS study material](https://www.nios.ac.in/online-course-material/secondary-courses.aspx) — open-schooling modules; the Culture and Environment modules are commonly recommended as concise syllabus-aligned coverage.
- [RBI Publications](https://www.rbi.org.in/) — monetary policy statements & annual report, for verifying economy current-affairs claims.
- [MEA (Ministry of External Affairs) briefs](https://www.mea.gov.in/) — primary-source material for International Relations answers.
- [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) — full text of the Constitution and Central Acts.
- [NCERT textbooks (free PDFs)](https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php) — the standard foundational reading across History, Geography, Polity, Economy, Science.

### General Notes from Aspirant Discussions

- A recurring theme across forums: **limit your toolkit** — one app per job (current affairs, notes, spaced repetition, test series) rather than collecting many overlapping apps.
- Primary government sources (PIB, PRS, NCERTs) are consistently flagged as underused relative to paid coaching material, despite being free and closer to how questions are actually framed.
- Newspaper + monthly compilation is the most repeated current-affairs workflow: read daily for understanding, revise from a single monthly consolidation rather than a year of scattered notes.

*As with the syllabus links above, verify any tool/site before relying on it heavily — free resources change ownership, shut down, or go behind paywalls over time.*
